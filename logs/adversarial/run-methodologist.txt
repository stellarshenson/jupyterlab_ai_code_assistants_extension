METHODOLOGIST ADVERSARY - THE RUN, NOT THE CODE
target: the adversarial review campaign that produced commit 212aeb8
        (jupyterlab_ai_code_assistants_extension, v1.0.8)
lens:   scientific-method integrity - can the test fail, does the verdict
        ladder span outcomes, was the bar pre-registered and honoured, can
        the controls move, are the metrics referenced to one thing

VERDICT: DO-NOT-SHIP (10 findings)

The campaign found real defects and fixed most of them. That is not in
question and is not what this review grades. What is graded is whether the
procedure that declared them found, fixed and closed could have returned the
opposite answer. On six of ten axes it could not.

The headline: the ship bar ("one clean round from all four lenses") was set
on a variable no lens computed from its own evidence, over a target the
previous round had rewritten, with an exclusion rule that makes the bar
reachable by writing a ledger entry instead of a fix. It was never met in 41
open-scope lens-rounds. The number that finally stopped the run - 11 of 75
defects fix-induced, six of nine causing fixes MINOR - is not reproducible
from the ledger it was derived from, undercounts by at least 8, and its
load-bearing half inverts when recounted.


================================================================================
1. FALSIFIABILITY - COULD THE VERDICT HAVE FAILED?
================================================================================

The bar, .claude/workflows/confirming-round-6-v1.0.7.js:159:

  "One clean round from all four lenses is the ship bar, and this is that
   round. ... set verdict SHIP only if you found none that matter."

"none that matter" is a free variable. Nothing in FINDINGS_SCHEMA
(:59-88) or anywhere else maps severity onto verdict. The consequence is
observable in the data, not hypothetical:

  logs/adversarial/wholerepo-architect-round5.txt:7   VERDICT: SHIP, six
      findings, finding 1 a MAJOR that the campaign then fixed as DEF-58
  logs/adversarial/wholerepo-architect-round6.txt:9   VERDICT: SHIP, "Five
      MINOR findings are listed below"; F1 was ACTED ON in the final wave
      (the three comment corrections)

So SHIP does not mean "nothing to fix", and the same finding set could have
been graded either way by the lens's own discretion. "Clean" has no
definition that the artefacts can be checked against.

Empirical reachability, all three campaigns, one line per lens-round:

  batch campaign  (pre-registered addendum bar)  11 reports,  3 SHIP
  colour campaign (open-scope bar)               21 reports,  0 SHIP
  whole-repo      (open-scope bar)               20 reports,  2 SHIP

  whole-repo per lens:  architect 2/5, bug-hunter 0/5,
                        slop-hunter 0/5, ux-designer 0/5

Both whole-repo SHIPs came from one lens. Three lenses never shipped in five
attempts each. Taking the point estimates, P(clean round) = 0. Taking the
most generous one-sided 95% upper bound for a lens observed 0/5 (0.451),
P(clean round) = 0.4 x 0.451^3 = 0.037, and P(at least one clean round in six)
= 0.20. The stop condition was a four-of-four simultaneous draw on an event
with a ~5% empirical base rate. It was not a bar; it was a lottery, and the
run bought six tickets.

The trivial classifier: an adversary that returns DO-NOT-SHIP unconditionally
scores 39/41 against the observed open-scope record. Nothing in the procedure
distinguishes the lenses from that classifier.

3. THE EXCLUSION RULE MAKES THE BAR GAMEABLE
================================================================================

.claude/workflows/confirming-round-6-v1.0.7.js:38

  "DEFECT LEDGER: docs/defects.md holds 74 closed and 1 open. Read it before
   reporting anything - a finding already in there is not a finding."

A known-open defect cannot be reported. Therefore a lens's verdict is
computed over (all defects MINUS all logged defects), and moving a finding
from unlogged to logged is operationally identical to fixing it, as far as
the bar is concerned.

The final wave logged seven new defects (DEF-76..DEF-82) and fixed two lines.
Under this rule a hypothetical round 7 could not report any of the seven. The
campaign ended one procedural step away from a clean round it could have
manufactured by writing prose.

CONFIRMED. Consequence: the ship bar does not measure defect count; it
measures unlogged defect count, and the campaign controls the ledger.

(d) THE LOAD-BEARING HALF INVERTS ON RECOUNT - THIS IS THE REAL CRACK

The inference that justified stopping is not the rate. It is "six of the nine
causing fixes were MINORs ... the worst offender was cosmetic ... fixing more
is what makes it worse". That claim requires MINOR fixes to be
disproportionately dangerous. Two tests:

  TEST 1 - against the base rate. The ledger's severity distribution is
  MINOR 49, MAJOR 19, HIGH 7, CRITICAL 4 (49/79 = 62% MINOR). Under a null in
  which fix-induction is independent of severity, E[MINOR among 9 causing
  fixes] = 5.6. Observed 6. P(>= 6 of 9) = 0.53. The observation is the
  median outcome of drawing nine fixes at random. A coin weighted by the
  ledger's own severity mix reproduces the headline exactly.

  TEST 2 - recount the causing fixes. Under the 19-entry set the causing
  fixes with a recorded severity are:

    MINOR    DEF-12, DEF-26, DEF-30, DEF-31, DEF-36, DEF-60   6
    MAJOR    DEF-18, DEF-33, DEF-35, DEF-37                   4
    HIGH     DEF-10                                           1
    CRITICAL DEF-32                                           1

  6 of 12 = 50% MINOR, against a 62% base rate. MINOR fixes spawn defects
  slightly BELOW their share of all fixes. The sign of the claim reverses.

CONFIRMED. Consequence: "six of nine were MINORs" is arithmetic, not
evidence. It cannot fail, and it does not survive its own ledger.

(e) THE "50% IN THE LAST WAVE" DENOMINATOR

The last wave is DEF-72..DEF-75 (n=4) or DEF-70..DEF-75 (n=6); the plan
does not say which. Under the plan's own table only DEF-74 and DEF-75 are
fix-induced, giving 2/4; DEF-73 must be counted to reach 3/6.

  2/4 = 50%  95% CI [7%, 93%]   p vs 14.7% base = 0.105
  3/6 = 50%  95% CI [12%, 88%]  p vs 14.7% base = 0.045

The confidence interval spans almost the entire unit interval under either
reading. The four-to-six entries are also a SUBSET of the 75, so "15%
campaign-wide vs 50% in the last wave" is not an independent trend - it is one
sample compared against a pool that contains it.

Plainly: the number that stopped the campaign is weaker than it was
presented. It was presented as a measurement with a decisive escalation; it
is an unreproducible prose scan, undercounting by 8, whose comparative half
is a six-sample estimate with a 76-point confidence interval and whose causal
half is at the base rate.

That said - and this matters - the DECISION was right for reasons the
number did not carry. Section 2's 85% figure is the sound version of the
same argument and the run never computed it.

(c) THE IMPACT-AGENT CONTROL CANNOT MOVE

No impact agent in the record ever upgraded a finding or confirmed a MAJOR.

CONFIRMED. Consequence: the severity ladder inflates or deflates nothing
consistently, and the one adjudication step in the campaign has no
demonstrated dynamic range and no artefact.

WHAT THE METHOD GOT RIGHT

Not a courtesy list. These are the parts that could have failed and didn't.

  verdict mapping and closing the scope. Three SHIPs in <= 4 rounds. This is

  a property of adversarial review.

- REFUTATION WAS BUILT IN AND IT KILLED THINGS. Two skeptics per lens
  (confirming-round-6-v1.0.7.js:149), instructed that "Refuted if the

  false and was corrected"), DEF-78 (:106, "rescoped by its impact agent").

  sweep with a control that moves across its whole range: OLD BAD 10/10 at
  3/6/10/20ms, NEW BAD 0/10 at all four, plus a no-injected-delay arm and a
  no-client arm. The criterion is exercised on both sides. Its only fault is
  the column its closure note left out (section 7c).

- DEF-52 IS A GENUINE DIFFERENTIAL TEST THAT FAILED ON FIRST RUN.
  docs/defects.md:242 - "a round-4 architect SUSPICION ... turned into a
